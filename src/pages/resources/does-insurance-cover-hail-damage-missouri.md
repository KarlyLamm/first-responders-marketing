## What is usually covered

Standard homeowners policies list hail as a named, covered peril. That typically includes:

- Roof shingles, underlayment and flashing damaged by hail
- Gutters, downspouts, vents and skylights
- Siding, window screens and sometimes window glass
- Interior damage from water that came in through hail damage

## What can reduce a payout

**Deductibles.** Some policies have a separate wind and hail deductible, often a percentage of your dwelling coverage rather than a flat amount. Look at your declarations page.

**Actual cash value roof coverage.** Some policies pay a depreciated value for older roofs instead of full replacement cost.

**Cosmetic damage exclusions.** Some policies exclude dents that do not affect function, such as on metal roofing.

**Late reporting.** Policies require prompt notice. Waiting a long time gives an insurer grounds to question whether damage came from that storm.

## How to tell if you have hail damage

Look for dents on gutters, downspouts, roof vents and the fins of your AC unit, and for shingle granules at downspout outlets. Damage on the roof itself is hard to see from the ground. ## How the claim goes

1. Get an inspection and photos of the damage.
2. Report the claim to your insurer.
3. An adjuster inspects. Have your contractor there.
4. The insurer approves a scope. You pay your deductible.
5. Hidden damage found during the job is submitted as a supplement.
