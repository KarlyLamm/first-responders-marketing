## 1. The type of water

Restoration standards sort water into three categories. **Clean water** from a supply line is the least expensive to handle. **Gray water**, like a washer or dishwasher discharge, needs more cleaning. **Black water**, like sewage or outside floodwater, requires removing porous materials it touched, which raises the cost.

## 2. How far it spread

Water travels under baseboards, into wall cavities and down through floors. A leak that reached one room costs far less than one that soaked through the ceiling into a finished basement.

## 3. How long it sat

When drying starts within the first day, carpet, drywall and wood can often be dried in place. After a few days, more has to be torn out and replaced, and mold remediation may be added.

## 4. How much gets rebuilt

Drying is one part of the bill. Replacing drywall, flooring, cabinets and paint is often the larger part.

## Who pays

Sudden, accidental water damage, like a burst pipe, is generally covered by homeowners insurance after your deductible.
